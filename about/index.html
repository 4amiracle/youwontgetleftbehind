How it started

On a couch, with my best friend, one night.

I kept going to tech conferences and hearing people talk about AI with a confidence and poise I didn't feel. I didn't want to become another expert. I wanted to be another voice in the mix, one that could bring people together at the community level.

My best friend nudged me to take the leap. So I downloaded Claude, paid for a monthly subscription, and started talking to it out loud, transcribing my thoughts and asking it to help me build websites for the things I care about.

That's how I'm learning Claude Code: by making personal projects I'm passionate about, so one day I can bring that confidence into my full-time work. This website is one of them. It's being built the same way, in real time, as I talk.

Who I am

A people person who ended up in tech.

I've worked in tech for the past four years, after a short stint in food and beverage. Through all of it, my work has been about people: diversity, equity, inclusion, and belonging.

Right now I'm most passionate about helping people in non-technical roles build skills with AI, and about giving us room to talk honestly about the anxiety and pressure we feel as humans working in tech. I want to get all of us into one room so we can help each other, teach each other, and let some of that fear go.

Why me

Because I'm not an expert.

I'm not going to tell you I know everything. I won't hand you the perfect prompt or a clever acronym for writing one.

What I will tell you is that it's a wild time to be alive, and you deserve to play. Figure things out. Make mistakes, break things, and build them back up, as long as you're keeping your data and yourself safe.

Eyes open

Curious, and honest about the cost.

Morning, afternoon, and night, AI is changing people's lives and livelihoods. It raises real questions about equity, social justice, energy, and sustainability. Learning these tools doesn't mean looking away from any of that.

Using tools that can feel at odds with what we believe will test our moral compass. Sometimes it will shift. Often it will hold, and we'll stay true to who we are. Both deserve a place in the conversation.

What I'm building

A community of people who've been touched by tech and still want to do good in the world.
