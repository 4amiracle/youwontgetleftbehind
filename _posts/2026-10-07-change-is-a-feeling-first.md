Most AI rollouts follow the same script: a tool gets picked, an announcement goes out, a training is scheduled. Then, quietly, almost nobody changes how they work.

It's not because people are lazy or resistant. It's because **change is a feeling before it's a process.**

## What people are actually feeling

When a new technology shows up at work, the questions in people's heads usually aren't about features. They sound more like:

- *Am I going to look stupid?*
- *Is this going to replace me?*
- *If I get good at this, does my old expertise still matter?*

Until those feelings get room to breathe, no amount of training slides will help.

> People don't resist change. They resist losing who they are.

## Three things I'm learning to do

### 1. Say the scary part out loud
If leaders don't name the fear, people assume the worst. Honest beats polished.

### 2. Make it safe to be a beginner
Create spaces where trying, failing, and asking "basic" questions is normal, even celebrated.

### 3. Start with one tiny habit
Not "transform your workflow." Instead: "Use it once this week to draft one email." Small wins build the confidence that big change needs.
